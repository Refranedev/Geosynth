Namibia GIS field guide

Every layer and every column in the project, explained in plain language. For each field you get what it means, what values are actually in the file, and whether it's worth using.

The basics first

Layer

One map dataset, like one spreadsheet where every row also has a shape on the map. Each .geojson file is one layer.

Row (feature)

One thing on the map: one town, one road segment, one patch of rock, one mineral find.

Field (column)

One piece of information recorded for every row, such as a name, a code or a length.

Geometry

The shape. Point = a dot. Line = a road, fault or dyke. Polygon = an area. “Multi” just means one row can be made of several pieces, like a region that includes islands.

Coordinate system (CRS)

How positions are written. WGS84 = latitude/longitude in degrees. UTM 33S = metres east and north, the usual grid for Namibia. Africa Albers = metres on a whole-of-Africa grid. QGIS converts between them automatically.

Empty (null)

Nothing was recorded. Some columns are empty in every single row. They were set up for later but never filled.

Geological time, as used in this data

Name in the dataRoughly whenIn plain words
Quaternary2.6 Ma – todayThe most recent period: today's sand dunes, river gravels and salt pans.
Neogene · Palaeogene (“Tertiary”, T)66 – 2.6 MaAfter the dinosaurs. Kalahari sands, Namib sandstone.
Cretaceous · Jurassic · Triassic (Mesozoic)252 – 66 MaThe dinosaur era. Africa and South America split apart and huge lava floods (Etendeka) poured out.
Permian · Carboniferous (Karoo)359 – 252 MaIce ages, then swamps and basins. Gives Namibia's Karoo rocks and coal.
Ordovician · Cambrian (Palaeozoic)541 – 443 MaLate-stage granites and the upper Nama sediments.
Namibian = Neoproterozoic1000 – 541 MaNamibia's own name for this span. The Damara mountain belt formed when continents collided. Hosts most copper.
Namaquan = Mesoproterozoic1600 – 1000 MaOld, heavily cooked and squeezed rocks of the south (Namaqua).
Kheisian = Palaeoproterozoic2500 – 1600 MaThe oldest rocks in the data, deep “basement” below everything else.

Ma = millions of years ago. Ages are approximate standard ranges; the data itself stores names, not numbers.

Supporting files

The files that aren't data

  • .qmd files (one per layer) are QGIS metadata sidecars, meant to hold a description, author, licence and dates. Nearly all are blank templates. Only Known Mineral Occurences.qmd (“Mineral occurrences and showings (GSN occurrence database and others)”) and Lithostratigraphic Units.qmd (“Lithostratigraphic and lithodemic units”) have a one-line description.
  • geo_lithostrat_units_by_age.qml is a saved QGIS style. It colours the geology layer by its age_name column into 19 legend classes, from “Quaternary — Namib Sand Sea…” down to “Kheisian (Palaeoproterozoic)…”. Load it on the Lithostratigraphic Units layer to get the classic age-coloured geology map.
  • .png files are finished map pictures exported from QGIS: Namibian Regions.png, Known Mineral Occurences of Namibia.png and Lithostratigraphic Map of Namibia.png. They're for viewing only and contain no data.
